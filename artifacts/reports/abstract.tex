We compare sequential C++, OpenMP, local MPI and CUDA implementations of
Monte Carlo pi estimation and a known multidimensional integral. Evaluation
separates estimator variance from runtime dispersion and tests interval
coverage under separated and repeated streams. Three shuffled rounds give
30 distinct seeds per configuration. At ten million samples, eight-worker
OpenMP and MPI median speedups are 3.98 and 3.34; CUDA reaches 68.00,
including different RNG-family work. A separate 100-trial CUDA study at
one billion samples gives coverage 98/100 with separated streams and 12/100
with repeated streams. Hardware counters support an FP64 arithmetic bottleneck
for the tested kernels, and sustained telemetry quantifies gross GPU-only
energy. This is an applied comparative study on one laptop; it does not
establish universal RNG independence or a new Monte Carlo algorithm.
